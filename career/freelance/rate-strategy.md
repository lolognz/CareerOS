# Freelance rate strategy v1

## Purpose and boundary

This file contains the global economic planning and decision framework for evaluating freelance opportunities, regardless of sourcing channel. It is career strategy, not canonical candidate knowledge, tax advice, or an exact tax calculator.

The billing and rate ranges below are approximate planning estimates. They must not be presented as exact fiscal results or guaranteed income. Every concrete opportunity requires its own evaluation using current tax rules, realistic utilization, and actual contract terms.

## Sustainable freelance net objective

The purpose of moving to freelance is a material economic improvement over stable employment, not merely higher gross billing.

The confirmed minimum target range is approximately EUR 4,000–5,000 of average sustainable monthly net disposable income across the full 12-month year. This is not a maximum; higher-paying opportunities remain desirable.

The sustainable net measure must include the effects of:

- vacation;
- holidays;
- non-billable administrative and commercial time;
- possible gaps between projects;
- income tax;
- self-employed Social Security contributions;
- normal professional expenses.

It must not be confused with net cash during a fully billed month. VAT is not personal income and must never be counted as such.

### Conditions supporting the lower end

A result closer to EUR 4,000 per month can be acceptable when the project offers strong non-monetary value, especially:

- long expected duration;
- high stability or predictability;
- fully remote work;
- strong technical interest;
- strong career-development value;
- good flexibility and working conditions.

### Conditions requiring the upper end

A result closer to EUR 5,000 per month should be required when the project is less attractive or carries more risk, for example:

- short or uncertain duration;
- lower stability;
- weaker career value;
- weaker technical interest;
- required presence or hybrid constraints;
- worse flexibility;
- higher client or project risk;
- worse general conditions.

No precise scoring formula is defined.

Actual opportunity evaluation must replace the reference scenario with the best available evidence about:

## Commercial rate policy

### Below EUR 50/hour

Normally reject. This rate does not provide enough margin over the minimum economic target under the current planning assumptions.

### EUR 50–55/hour

Consider only for an exceptional project. It should normally require strong duration, stability, remote work, technical interest, career value, flexibility, or another meaningful advantage.

### EUR 55–60/hour

Serious consideration zone. It can justify moving to freelance depending on project conditions.

### EUR 60–65/hour

Preferred and strong target zone.

### EUR 65–70/hour

Economically very attractive and approximately aligned with the upper sustainable-net objective under the reference assumptions.

### Above EUR 70/hour

Clearly attractive economically. Project quality, risk, duration, and contract conditions remain relevant.

### Floor versus mathematical minimum

- **Approximate hourly-rate range corresponding to the low planning billing target at 1,500 billable hours:** EUR 50–53/hour.
- **Current commercial floor for normal positioning:** approximately EUR 55/hour.

The commercial floor intentionally sits above the mathematical planning range to leave margin for uncertainty. The EUR 55/hour commercial floor is a policy for normal positioning, not an absolute never-break rule; exceptional project value may justify a deliberate exception.

## Opportunity evaluation principle

Hourly rate must never be evaluated in isolation. Every concrete opportunity should eventually be evaluated using at least:

- hourly, daily, or monthly rate;
- expected billable hours;
- expected duration;
- termination and cancellation conditions;
- remote, hybrid, or on-site requirements;
- flexibility;
- payment terms;
- client and project risk;
- technical interest;
- career-development value;
- expected gap risk after project end.

A lower hourly rate may outperform a higher one when duration and utilization are materially better.

## Remaining uncertainty and project-specific inputs

- Exact annual restaurant-voucher amount.
- Exact annual employee net compensation.
- Actual professional operating expenses as a freelancer.
- Actual deductible expenses.
- Exact tax treatment and rules in force when a future project is evaluated.
- Actual billable hours and utilization.
- Actual downtime and gaps between projects.
- Project-specific contract, termination, cancellation, and payment conditions.
- Any personal or family circumstances relevant to an accurate future tax estimate.

These unknowns should be resolved only when needed and from current, reliable inputs. This strategy must remain a planning framework rather than evolve into an implicit tax calculator.
